# warm: the judges' notes

In each judge's text, A and B are the images as that judge saw them; the line before says which was yours.

## Our screens against the current ones (the goal): your direction 4, the current screens 0, same 0

- **Judge 1** (A was your direction) picked your direction. The two are nearly identical; the differences are small and all favour A. A keeps a filled surface on its interactive controls: the owner picker on the ticket, the Claude Code/sonnet/high pickers on the agent and Save in both editors. B strips these to bare text, so they lose their affordance, and B's Save reads as a label next to a bordered Discard, which inverts the hierarchy. A also keeps the queued run cards on the same baseline as the running ones (an empty track at the same position) and leaves a gap between the inline action buttons and the stage column. B lets "Waiting for a runner" drop below the running cards' progress line, butts the Approve and Retry buttons against "Sign-off" and "Implement", and cuts off the plan list at the bottom of the ticket page where A fades it out.
  - the current screens's gap: Give every interactive control a resting surface: a filled chip behind the owner picker and the Claude Code/sonnet/high pickers, and a filled Save button in the editor headers. Bare text with a chevron, or a bare "Save", does not read as clickable.
  - your direction's flaw: Replace the dashed outline on Discard in the editor headers (screens 7 and 10) with a solid quiet ghost button. At the moment it looks like a disabled control or a drop zone, and it pulls more weight than the Save button beside it.
  - Broken: B, screens 5 and 8: the ticket page's scrolling plan list is cut off at the bottom edge, with "Swap the optimistic message for the server's copy" sliced through the text and no fade. B, screens 1 and 2: the Approve and Retry buttons in the Your move rows sit flush against the "Sign-off" and "Implement" column text. B, screens 1 and 2: in the queued run cards (NW-23, NW-24), "Waiting for a runner" is out of line with the matching row in the running cards beside them. A: nothing broken.

## The twins against Linear (secondary): your direction 0, Linear 4, same 0

- **Judge 1** (A was your direction) picked Linear. B uses specific glyphs and A does not. B has a funnel for Filter, sliders for Display, sort-direction arrows for Ordering and a git-PR icon for links. A uses the same stacked-lines icon for Filter, Display, Grouping, view toggles and the toolbar, so its controls are hard to tell apart. B also lays out text better: titles truncate later, the branch name stays on one line, and its controls look like real inputs (bordered selects, a bordered segmented List/Board control).
